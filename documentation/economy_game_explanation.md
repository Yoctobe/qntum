My note:
CYCLE 1 — LOW RATES
Low rates → credit is cheap → people borrow and spend → demand rises → prices rise (inflation) → currency weakens → exports boom because your goods are cheap for foreigners → economy overheats → Fed is forced to raise rates.
CYCLE 2 — HIGH RATES
High rates → borrowing expensive → spending drops → inflation cools → currency strengthens → exports slow down → government issues bonds and investors buy them (good yield) → but economy slows, unemployment rises → government now needs unemployment to fall to restore tax revenue and spending → so they start cutting rates again.
And the wheel turns back to Cycle 1.
The one thing you added that's critical: the government is always in deficit in the background, so every cycle they're issuing bonds — cheap in cycle 1, expensive in cycle 2. High rate cycles are painful for governments because they pay more interest on every bond, which widens the deficit, which forces more bond issuance — the debt grows fastest exactly when rates are highest.
That's the full loop.
